# Rules: drawing

Generated from the decks by `_scripts/playbook_pack.py render`; never edit it. To change a
rule, change its deck row.

## Sources and records

- **R-DRW-2** (eval: deck case only (no eval yet)) Every number in a sketch names its source in the sketch's comment. When no photograph was used, the comment and the drawing's notes say so. *Why:* A sketch that looks exact but rests on a guess passes the guess on to every stage after it. Reject when: A size with no named source, or a drawing from no photograph that does not say so.
- **R-DRW-10** (eval: deck case only (no eval yet)) Claude never fills the "Checked" cell. It stays empty until a person has checked the drawing against its sources. *Why:* A checked box that nobody checked is a false record. Reject when: An approval cell filled when no person checked the drawing.
- **R-DRW-11** (eval: deck case only (no eval yet)) A dimension that disagrees with the research goes back to the sketch. Never move a dimension line or its value by hand. *Why:* Dimensions are measured from the sketch's geometry; a hand-moved value is a drawing that lies. Reject when: A dimension value typed in, or a line placed by hand.
- **R-DRW-21** (eval: deck case only (no eval yet)) The wireframe and the shaded model turned through 360 degrees are review views of the model, not drawings: they carry no dimensions, title block or scale, and are read to judge shape. The drawing is made from the same geometry, so a change of shape is made once, in the model. *Why:* In CAD the model is the master and the drawings are derived from it; a review render dressed as a drawing is a second record that can disagree. Reject when: Dimensions or a title block on a review render, or a drawing with geometry of its own.

## Views and lines

- **R-DRW-4** (eval: deck case only (no eval yet)) A drawing is first-angle orthographic projection: the side elevation as the main view, the end elevation from the nose on its right, the plan below it, an isometric view, and the first-angle symbol (the cone's narrow end away from its circles). *Why:* It is the UK and European convention, and a reader who knows it reads the part without being told which view is which. Reject when: A third-angle layout, a view out of its place, or no projection symbol.
- **R-DRW-5** (eval: deck case only (no eval yet)) Line types and weights follow ISO 128: visible outlines and the section's cut thick (0.5 mm on A3); hidden edges thin and dashed; centre and cutting-plane lines thin chain (long, short); dimension, extension and hatching lines thin (0.25 mm); the frame 0.7 mm. Dimension lines carry arrowheads, and extension lines leave a gap at the part. *Why:* Weight says what a line is before its dash does; one weight for all of them flattens the drawing. Reject when: Two kinds of line at the same weight and dash, or a weight off the 2:1 pair.
- **R-DRW-16** (eval: deck case only (no eval yet)) Draw each view from the model's own geometry: the hard edges where faces meet at an angle, and the silhouette as that view sees it, so a smooth part (a foil, a tyre, a hull) still has its outline. A face within 3 degrees of edge-on counts as edge-on. *Why:* A smooth part drawn by its hard edges alone vanishes, and a thin part whose faces flip side to side between triangles draws outlines that are not there. Reject when: A smooth part with no outline in a view, or a silhouette line across a flat face.

## Sheet and scale

- **R-DRW-6** (eval: deck case only (no eval yet)) A drawing prints at a standard scale on A3 (1:1, 1:2, 1:5, 1:10, 1:20 and so on). The margins grow to fit it; the drawing never stretches to fill the sheet. *Why:* A drawing at 1:13 cannot be measured with a scale rule. Reject when: A scale off the ISO 5455 series.
- **R-DRW-7** (eval: deck case only (no eval yet)) The sheet follows ISO 5457 (frame with a 20 mm filing margin and 10 mm elsewhere, a grid reference border of 50 mm fields from the centre lines, numbers along and letters up with no I or O, centring marks) and its title block ISO 7200: the eight mandatory fields (legal owner, identification number of at most 16 characters, date of issue, sheet number, title, creator, approval person, document type) plus revision, status and paper, the identification number bottom right. Scale and projection are not title-block fields: they sit by the projection symbol. Lettering heights come from ISO 3098's series (1.8, 2.5, 3.5, 5, 7 mm). *Why:* That is what a proper drawing sheet carries, and "the lamp is at D6" is something two people can point at. Reject when: A missing mandatory field, the scale in the title block, or a grid letter I or O.
- **R-DRW-9** (eval: deck case only (no eval yet)) A drawing is monochrome: ink lines on the ground, told apart by weight, dashes and hatching. The accent colour is used only when a drawing is put on display to point at one thing, and then nowhere else on it. *Why:* A drawing is a record to measure against; colour on it reads as meaning that is not there. Reject when: Colour on a drawing at rest, or more than one accented thing on display.
- **R-DRW-17** (eval: deck case only (no eval yet)) Lay a sheet out in paper millimetres: the gaps between views and the room for dimensions and names are fixed on paper, and the views take the largest standard scale that fits what is left. A section takes the largest standard scale its own space holds, and the isometric view a standard scale of its own; a view not at the sheet's scale says its scale under its name. *Why:* Spacing set in the model's units makes a long part print small with paper wasted, and a section fixed at twice the scale crowds its labels. Reject when: A view at a scale off the series, or at another scale than the sheet's with no label.

## Dimensions and sections

- **R-DRW-12** (eval: deck case only (no eval yet)) A drawing of a physical part is dimensioned as ISO 129-1 does it: each size once, in mm with no unit (the sheet says MM once), the value above its line and along it, read from the bottom or the right; extension lines leave a 1 mm gap at the part and run 2 mm past the line; arrowheads 3 mm at 15 degrees. No variables table: names with a table are for UI blueprints, where the values are tokens that change. *Why:* A machinist reads the number where the size is; a table beside it is clutter. Reject when: A size written twice, a unit on a value, or a names-and-table drawing of a physical part.
- **R-DRW-13** (eval: deck case only (no eval yet)) A part whose shape is its point (a wing element) gets section A-A: a cutting-plane line on the plan with arrows in the viewing direction, and the cut profile drawn at twice the scale, hatched at 45 degrees. The sketch's `cut` puts the plane where every element is cut cleanly. *Why:* The elevations show a wing element edge-on and dashed; only a section shows its profile. Reject when: A profile part that has no section, or a section that cuts two elements into one.
- **R-DRW-19** (eval: deck case only (no eval yet)) A part that occurs twice, or as its mirror image, has one drawing: the parts list gives its quantity, and the GA notes the mirrored copy. A round part is dimensioned once by its diameter (with the diameter sign) and its width, not as a length in every view. *Why:* Two drawings of one part drift apart, and a wheel's size written along each view says one thing three times. Reject when: Two drawings of one part, or a diameter written as a length in more than one view.

## Drawing sets

- **R-DRW-18** (eval: deck case only (no eval yet)) A whole object is a set of drawings. Sheet 1 is the general arrangement (ISO 10209): the whole object, dimensioned only overall (length, wheelbase, height, tracks, width), each item named by a balloon (ISO 6433) whose number is its item in the parts list on sheet 2 (ISO 7573: item, title, quantity, drawing number, revision, document type). Each item then has its own assembly or detail drawing under its own number. *Why:* One drawing of a whole object cannot carry every part's sizes, and a part drawn alone loses where it sits; the balloons and the list tie the set together. Reject when: A GA with part sizes on it, a balloon with no item, or an item with no drawing.
- **R-DRW-20** (eval: deck case only (no eval yet)) A balloon sits clear of every part and every other balloon, its leader ends in a dot on the item it names, and no two leaders cross. Where the placed balloon lands on another item, the sketch gives a point on its own item for it. *Why:* A balloon that sits on the wrong part names the wrong part. Reject when: A balloon on another item, or two leaders crossing.

## Keeping one source

- **R-DRW-15** (eval: deck case only (no eval yet)) Where an ISO drawing standard covers it, the drawing follows the standard, because each one is settled practice. The register below says which part of the kit holds each, and what is not yet followed. *Why:* The standards exist because generations of drawings were misread without them. Reject when: A drawing practice a standard covers, done another way with no line in the register.
- **R-DRW-22** (eval: deck case only (no eval yet)) These rules live in this vault as the playbook `Playbooks/drawing`; a project receives a copy of its SKILL, RULES, LOOK and STANDARDS files by sync and never edits the copy. A project keeps its own rules for how it builds the object apart, in its own playbook. *Why:* One source, reusable by every project that draws, and nothing forks quietly. Reject when: A project's copy that differs from the playbook.
