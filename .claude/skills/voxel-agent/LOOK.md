# Visual review

Use a numbered atlas with both successful and awkward seeds. Keep terrain and object
choices visible together. The desktop picture and phone picture are separate evidence.
Record the exact public seed, layout seed, engine version and screenshot path.

- Can a reader identify the landform without its label: valley, dune, alpine ridge,
  cave opening, island, archipelago or a mainland slice?
- Does a mainland edge cut through the actual terrain and vegetation, rather than a
  water border added to every scene?
- Do outer sides read as an irregular, stepped geological edge rather than four tall,
  flat slab faces? Check from multiple yaw angles and inspect the underside for holes.
- Do subcomponents have distinct silhouettes and a coherent local selection, rather than
  the same five props repeated through each biome?
- Are empty wilderness, a small camp and a dense settlement all represented?
- Do buildings vary in number, site, dimensions, roof and orientation?
- Do forests have enough canopy density to read as forests, while arid terrain stays sparse?
- Does the transition look like an ecotone, with continuous relief and local vegetation,
  rather than an unrelated colour split pasted onto one platform?
- Do skeletons read as bones, tents as shelters, and caves as spaces under rock?
- Can every roof, tree canopy, overhang and sail be followed without a random intersection?
- Do paths leave usable entrances? Are wet crossings honestly absent when no bridge exists?
- Does sunlight describe the faces and heights? Does the seed's rest view still read at dusk?
- After a drag, can the scene be inspected from another side without a camera jump?
- At default zoom, do the tallest peak and outer cut edges fit at every tested angle?
- Do the phone view, WebGL fallback and reduced-motion view retain the same composition?

A geometric pass does not settle recognition, clutter or taste. File an uncertain answer
with its seed and evidence instead of turning it into a machine-approved claim.

- Does canopy have an understory, with coherent gaps and enough small groundcover on slopes?
- Are supplies related to a camp, garden, farm, forge or settlement, and fossils/arches rare enough to read as discoveries?
- Do wildlife proportions and botanical shapes remain recognizable in close-ups, rather than only in labels?
- Does each Explore option focus accepted geometry? Can pan/pinch/close zoom reveal detail, with a usable whole-world reset?
- Do the model sheet and reference card support each stated visual claim? Keep any remaining primitive silhouettes or finite-world limits explicit.

- Does water read as ripples and shoreline foam rather than a regularly stamped grid? Check that submerged beds are not treated as banks and lava/ice do not acquire white foam.
